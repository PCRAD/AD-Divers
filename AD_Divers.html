Skills, specialties & places to start

Quick dive refresher.

Use this as a planning checklist, not dive training. Choose sites and conditions within your certification, experience, health, and local operator briefing.

Agree on the dive plan, limits, signals, entry and exit, gas plan, and lost-buddy procedure. Use the equipment and buddy-check sequence you were trained in; resolve any issue before entering the water.

Places to research: Bonaire has many shore-entry sites and local operators for guided refresher dives. Conditions and access vary by site.

Build relaxed, controlled movement and protect the bottom and marine life. Ask an instructor for a buoyancy check when changing exposure suit, tank, or equipment; make small adjustments and follow your training for ascents and stops.

Places to research: Bonaire and the calm reef sites around Roatán offer operator-led skills refreshers; check current conditions and entry requirements.

Use nitrox only with the required training. Analyze and verify the cylinder according to your course and operator procedure, set your computer to the analyzed mix, and stay within the depth and exposure limits you were taught.

Places to research: Bonaire and Cozumel have many dive operators; confirm nitrox availability, training, and cylinder procedures before booking.

A negative entry is not a shortcut or default. Use an entry method only when your training, operator briefing, sea state, equipment, and buddy plan support it. If you are not comfortable or conditions change, tell the crew and choose another plan.

Places to research: Cozumel boat operators sometimes plan current-assisted entries. Ask about the entry, exit, and alternatives; do not attempt a negative entry without prior instruction and practice.

Current strength and direction can change quickly. Review the briefing, stay with your buddy and guide, use the agreed pickup procedure, and skip the dive if the conditions exceed your experience.

Places to research: Palau's Blue Corner and Cozumel are known for current-influenced diving. They can be demanding; book with a local operator and follow site-specific limits.

Use a reef hook only where the operator explicitly permits it and after learning the correct method. Never attach to living coral or use one to compensate for poor buoyancy. Follow local conservation rules.

Places to research: Some Palau operators offer guided current dives at Blue Corner and explain if a hook is appropriate for that day's conditions.

Wreck exteriors and wreck penetration are different risk levels. Do not enter an overhead environment without the specific training, equipment, gas planning, and team procedures for that penetration.

Places to research: Chuuk Lagoon (often called Truk Lagoon) is famous for wrecks. Many sites are deep or penetrative; choose dives strictly within your qualifications and guide plan.

Cavern, cave, and wreck penetration require distinct training and equipment. Never pass a cavern's daylight/route limits or enter a cave without recognized cave-diving certification and a qualified team.

Places to research: The Yucatán cenotes near Tulum have guided cavern experiences; ask whether a dive stays in the cavern zone and match it to your certification.

Review light, communication, navigation, and buddy procedures before dark. Fluoro dives use dedicated lights and viewing filters; they are a specialty experience, not a reason to touch or disturb marine life.

Places to research: Bonaire has popular guided night dives; a few local operators offer fluorescence trips. Confirm equipment, conditions, and availability in advance.

Move slowly, maintain excellent buoyancy, and keep hands, fins, and camera gear off the bottom. Ask your guide how to observe small creatures without moving or handling them.

Places to research: Lembeh Strait in Indonesia and Anilao in the Philippines are well-known macro destinations; seasonal life and conditions vary.

A dry suit changes buoyancy and thermal planning. Get appropriate training, use suitable undergarments, check seals and valves, and dive within your recent cold-water experience.

Places to research: Silfra in Iceland is a cold, clear-water dry-suit destination; operators require specific preparation and conditions can be strenuous.

Cage viewing is generally a surface activity rather than a scuba dive. Choose a licensed operator with clear animal-welfare and safety practices; follow their instructions and never enter the water outside the approved setup.

Places to research: Gansbaai, South Africa is a known cage-viewing area. Confirm current local regulations, season, and operator practices.

Depth and altitude change dive planning. Stay within your certification and computer limits, use conservative profiles, and get site-specific instruction for high-altitude diving. Do not treat a refresher as a substitute for a specialty course.

Places to research: Cozumel's walls attract experienced divers; Lake Tahoe has high-altitude dive operations. Ask a local instructor about prerequisites and conditions.

Review entry/exit points, surf, surge, current, visibility, and an abort plan with a local professional. If access or visibility is outside your experience, choose a sheltered alternative or cancel.

Places to research: Bonaire is known for shore-diving access; select a site matched to the day's surf and your experience rather than assuming every entry is easy.

Keep buoyancy and awareness ahead of the shot. Maintain distance, do not chase, feed, touch, or block wildlife, and follow protected-area rules and your guide's site briefing.

Places to research: Raja Ampat and the Coral Triangle offer remarkable biodiversity; use local guides and marine-park rules to plan responsible encounters.

Review emergency procedures, surface-support signals, lost-buddy actions, and computer guidance with a qualified instructor. Keep certification current, maintain equipment, and follow your training for surface intervals and repetitive dives.

Where to practice: Use a controlled training site with a certified instructor before relying on any skill in open water.
